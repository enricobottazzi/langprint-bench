Two years ago, I was sitting on a packed metro train in Tokyo. Several passengers were absorbed in their books, and I found myself wondering whether those books would be shorter in Japanese than in English. My first guess was yes. Japanese uses two phonetic scripts and thousands of kanji, while English builds words from 26 letters. Take the word “cat.” In English it takes three letters. In Japanese, a single character will do: 猫.

This episode motivated me to ask a broader question: how much space does the same message occupy on a printed page across languages?

The results are striking. Russian takes almost twice as much printed space as Simplified Chinese for the same text. Across 200 passages in 10 domains, Chinese used about 35% less space than the ten-language average, while Russian used 28% more. Arabic (−30%), Hindi (−23%), and Japanese (−11%) also came in below average. English was slightly above (+6%), followed by Italian and Swahili (+15% each), Spanish (+16%), and German (+20%).

The heatmap on page 2 suggests that topic makes little difference to each language’s relative page space, though the nature and environment passages show the most variation across languages. Details on the methodology used to source the passages, translate them and score them under a fixed typography are available on Github.

Pick a passage and scroll through the ten books to see how much space it takes in each language. Shorter versions leave some pages blank so the next passage starts on the same page in every book. An asterisk (*) beside a title marks the language the passage was originally written in. The Arabic edition reads from right to left, so the next page is on the left.

On the Tokyo metro, I wondered how many pages those commuters had to turn. Now I wonder how long it took them to read those pages. Could someone reading George Orwell’s 1984 in Simplified Chinese finish it faster than someone reading it in Russian, with the same understanding of the story? A larger set of symbols can make a text more compact, but what does that mean for the reader? Imagine a writing system with a single symbol for an entire novel. How long would it take to learn everything that symbol stood for? How much of a story a reader can take in per minute is a question for another experiment.
